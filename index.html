    Stratum I

    The Founder

    In 2001 he started a theatre company named for the art of building something new out of whatever is at hand.

    Jeffrey Carpenter grew up in Pittsburgh, trained at NYU's Tisch School of the Arts, and came home to found Bricolage Production Company. It started as a home for new plays and readings. Over time it became something harder to describe: a company that builds alternatives to sitting in the dark and watching.

    The turn came while working on the U.S. premiere of Teatro de los Sentidos' Echo of the Shadow. Travelers wandered dreamlike chambers looking for a lost shadow, and the company never really came back out. Since then the work has asked one question: what if the audience wasn't watching the story, but inside it?

    At Bricolage you aren't an audience member. You're a participant, cast in the narrative and responsible for your part. The company supplies roughly eighty percent of the experience and trusts you with the rest. Physical safety comes first, consent is built in, and a safe word always ends it.

    He runs the company with his wife and co-artistic director, Tami Dixon. After nearly twenty years in a downtown Cultural District storefront, Bricolage moved to Community Forge in Wilkinsburg, a former elementary school now becoming a creative workshop for the neighborhood.
